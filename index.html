No. 01Brand Identity

BBH ships its zag in three free typefaces

Studio Drama for BBH

BBH's first identity refresh in 44 years pairs three custom typefaces (one per founder) with motion-led "Zag" glyphs that warp any line of copy into a brand asset. Made open-source via Google Fonts on day one — 4M downloads already — which is the actual strategic move: distribute the system, let the world write in your voice.

No. 02Brand Identity

Order builds a logo around what's missing

Order for the Frank Lloyd Wright Building Conservancy

A 4×4 grid of squares with one missing in the center — the "void" left when a Wright building is lost, plus a callback to the red square Wright signed his drawings with. A clinic in conceptual identity for legacy clients: the mark is the argument, no explainer copy required.

No. 03Brand Identity

FutureBrand draws an alphabet from the Amazon

FutureBrand São Paulo for the Brazilian Legal Amazon

Satellite imagery of the Amazon River and its tributaries traced into a typographic system, plus a "Feito de Amazônia" provenance seal for makers across the region's nine states. The case study to keep in your back pocket for any place-branding or community-co-designed identity pitch — the materials are literally the territory.

No. 04Art Direction

The Met argues fashion is art, then proves it

The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Costume Art exhibition

A spring exhibition pairing garments from The Met's archive with the artworks they echo, launched with the "Fashion is Art" Met Gala dress code as essentially a curatorial press release. Reference for any cultural-institution client: the right launch moment can do more for an exhibition's reach than the catalog ever will.

Three Hearts rum bottles a personal mythology

Mardo for Three Hearts (Johnny Depp)

A skull, a lightning bolt, and three hearts — each symbol load-bearing for Depp's biography — turned into a single integrated bottle architecture. Worth studying as the celebrity-spirits genre's most disciplined recent example: every glyph earns its place; nothing is just decoration.

No. 06Packaging

Sierra retires its sombrero

Stock Spirits — in-house redesign

The Sierra tequila line debuts a 2026 redesign that finally drops the brand's decades-old sombrero-cap motif — long the visual punchline, finally retired. A live case study for any heritage client weighing whether to retire a familiar-but-cringey identity asset: the new system carries the equity without the wince.

No. 07Campaign

Grey Goose books Heidi Klum and the Devil

Grey Goose × The Devil Wears Prada 2 — Bacardi global campaign

A theatrical co-marketing program — limited-edition bottle, a signature gold-dusted espresso martini, Heidi Klum starring, pop-ups and in-theater activations — built around the film's May 1 premiere. Solid template for spirits clients pitching co-promotions: anchor on a hero product (the cocktail), not the bottle alone.

No. 08Packaging

Aperol fits the spritz into a slim can

Aperol — global RTD launch

The full Aperol Spritz ritual, compressed into a 250ml slim can: 5% ABV, the same orange wash, designed for parks and trains rather than terraces. Worth tracking for any beverage client thinking about RTD entries — heritage brands win this category by ritual-preservation, not novelty.

No. 09Typography

NYCxDesign turns 14 with a bold "x"

NYCxDesign — 14th anniversary festival identity

The city's annual design week leans into a single-character system — the "x" doing all the heavy lifting across signage, web, social, and printed maps from May 14-20. Useful precedent for any cultural client whose identity needs to flex across 100+ partner events: pick one element you can defend, let everything else relax.

Smith & Diction Medium · Case Study · ~15 min

Branding Superhuman (and Grammarly and Coda)

Mike and Chara Smith walk through their work on three of the most-watched productivity brands, showing how each project required a different category posture — premium tool, foundational utility, and editor-collaborator. The actual workings of strategic positioning at a small studio, laid out in plain language; useful for any pitch where "what kind of brand are we" needs an answer fast.

Creative Bloq Editorial · Analysis · ~6 min

Pentagram's controversial new website could change the AI art debate

A close read of Pentagram's new site and the deliberate position it takes on AI-generated imagery — provocative because it isn't a position you'd expect from a legacy studio. Required reading before your next "should we use AI for this" conversation with a corporate client: Pentagram's argument is more nuanced than either side wants it to be.

Debbie Millman Design Matters · Podcast · ~70 min

Steven and Nicholas Heller on a life in design and New York

Father-and-son episode: Steven Heller (forever-prolific design writer) and his son Nicholas (the @NewYorkNico documentarian) talking about taste, attention, and a city that taught them both how to look. Closest thing to a masterclass in observing the visual world — and a reminder that "developing taste" is just a synonym for paying better attention.

Listen on Design Matters →
David Rager iF Design · Future of XYZ · ~30 min

Inside NASA's brand — David Rager on selling space exploration

NASA's Creative Director on what it means to steward one of the world's most-loved visual identities — and what changes when the audience is "every taxpayer who isn't sure why we're still doing this." A useful frame whenever a client's brand has to do persuasion and pride at the same time; rare to hear someone do it from inside a government agency.
