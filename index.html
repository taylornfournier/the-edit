No. 01Brand Identity

4Creative: 130 hands, one rotating logo

4Creative — in-house, Channel 4

Every member of the 130-person team drew their own ‘4C’; the identity rotates through all of them randomly, and the visual grammar cuts fragments from decades of iconic Channel 4 shows — Bake Off, Peep Show, Educating Yorkshire. A direct lesson in participatory identity design: when the process is the message, the work explains itself.

No. 02Brand Identity

OFFF grew its identity in a lab

Uncommon Creative Studio for OFFF Barcelona 2026

Uncommon swabbed doorknobs and cups at designer mixer events, cultured the biological traces, and built the entire visual system — including the custom typeface Hyphae, named for fungal thread structures — from the resulting moulds. A conference identity literally grown from its attendees’ presence. The brief that redefines what a brief can be.

No. 03Typography

Hellcare makes doctors’ scrawl legible — barely

Parker Jones & Rajshree Saraf

Jones and Saraf studied how handwriting degrades at maximum speed, then reverse-engineered the chaos into a consistent type system — controlled line weight, rhythm, and x-height intact, illegibility preserved. A strong lesson in using a conceptual anchor to define the aesthetic rather than hunting for a style and retrofitting it to an idea.

No. 04Brand Identity

Stoëmp builds brands that age on purpose

Stoëmp Studio — Ghent

The Ghent studio — co-founded by Wojciech and Gaetano, with strategist Thomas De Vylder — argues that a brand system is only sustainable if it’s rooted in something beyond trend: “built on clarity, structure, and intent, it can evolve without losing itself.” Worth reading before your next strategy session as a reminder that the brief precedes the moodboard, not the other way around.

No. 05Brand Identity

Copas makes constraint the whole canvas

Addison Copas — Virginia

Copas calls text-type design “a severely confined art” — and then proceeds to thrive inside those constraints, producing wordmarks that feel inevitable rather than designed. His core argument: freedom is the enemy of distinctiveness. Watch his work to recalibrate your instinct to reach for a typeface before you’ve settled on a concept.

No. 06Brand Identity

America finally has a Chief Brand Architect

Peter Arnell — US National Design Studio

Peter Arnell — DKNY, Samsung, the infamous Tropicana redesign — has been appointed to give the US government a unified visual identity from the Eisenhower Executive Office Building. Whatever your read on the politics: brand is now officially a government function. Worth watching for what it reveals about how the design industry will be asked to engage with public institutions at scale.

No. 07Brand Identity

Center gives Quip a typeface to grow up in

Center & Burn Type for Quip

Ten years after launch, the brand that reinvented toothbrush subscription finally got an identity that could hold a full product ecosystem. Center worked with Burn Type on quip Sans — mirrored letterforms, Bauhaus-influenced geometry — and rebuilt the entire system around it. Useful case study for any DTC client asking when to re-platform: when the portfolio has outgrown the launch identity.

No. 08Brand Identity

FLUORO stitches spray paint into the circuit board

FLUORO® for Doof — Edinburgh

A dynamic identity for the Scottish hard dance label pairs spray-painted Saltire energy with a throwback digital font that nods to early techno optimism — a system that moves differently across a lineup poster, a tote, and a stage backdrop without needing a grid to hold it. Prime reference for any music or culture client where the genre’s energy must live inside the letterforms.

No. 09Campaign

Chanel’s cast is the campaign

Chanel — Spring/Summer 2026 Eyewear

Five faces — Nicole Kidman, G-Dragon, Lily-Rose Depp, Pedro Pascal, Ayo Edebiri — each from a different cultural hemisphere, sharing a single frame. The campaign doesn’t try to reduce them to a common denominator; the tension between the cast is the point. Worth studying for any multi-talent campaign brief where the instinct is to art-direct personalities into homogeneity.

Reading.

Four things to chew on this week
Elizabeth Goodspeed It’s Nice That · Column · ~8 min read

“When more designers write, it changes how design gets talked about”

Goodspeed argues that when practitioners write about design — not critics, not journalists — the conversation shifts from aesthetics to stakes. Essential reading for any designer considering whether to start a newsletter, pitch a column, or spend thirty minutes writing up a project; the argument is both permission and instruction.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Debbie Millman Design Matters · Podcast · ~70 min

Manoush Zomorodi on Body Electric — screens, bodies, and what we lose

Zomorodi joins Millman to discuss the physical and psychological consequences of screen-centred life — and what happens to creativity when the body is no longer in the room. A useful frame for any design conversation about attention: what we design for and what we design against are sometimes the same object.

Listen on Design Matters →
PRINT Magazine Essay · ~8 min read

OFFF Barcelona: Why We Still Show Up

A quiet meditation on what design conferences are actually for — not the talks, but the corridors, the casual collisions, the unscheduled conversations — and why the OFFF crowd keeps returning despite knowing the roster months ahead. Quietly makes the case that the most irreplaceable thing in a virtual-first industry is proximity.
