No. 01Art Direction

Los Cabbalos turns dance into a visual system

Los Cabbalos for Public Records

The Buenos Aires studio builds Public Records’ new social identity from ritual and Kabbalah rather than nightlife’s usual speech-bubble clichés, pairing a ceremonial serif with loose, block-print figures caught mid-movement. A sharp reference for treating an entire social system, not just a logo, as the deliverable.

No. 02Campaign

Horniman Museum forages its own alphabet from nature

Anatomy for the Horniman Museum and Gardens

For the museum’s 125th year, Anatomy skips an off-the-shelf typeface and instead forages an entire letterset from the Horniman’s own gardens and collections, animating each character at a lower frame rate for a handmade, stop-motion feel. A model for how a bespoke type system can carry a campaign’s whole emotional register, not just its headlines.

No. 03Brand Identity

Open Point chisels civic trust into a wordmark

20(Something) for Open Point

20(Something) borrows the chiselled gravitas of a museum wordmark for Open Point, a merged civic-engagement platform that has to read as rigorous to planning officers and warm to the public consulted about a new roundabout. Worth studying for how confidently a brand can lift cues from an unrelated category — museums, not government — and still land exactly right.

No. 04Packaging

Makebardo tapes together a Cairo cafe’s identity

Makebardo for E11VN

Makebardo wraps a Cairo cafe-and-music-venue with a gaffer-tape-and-tote-bag illustration system so loose and charming it nearly rescues a genuinely difficult brand name. A good case study in how far a distinctive, flexible illustration language can carry an identity when the naming and positioning upstream were out of the designers’ hands.

No. 05Brand Identity

Fjord swaps wellness pastels for industrial cold plunge

DutchScot for Fjord

For a floating sauna and cold-plunge experience in Sausalito, DutchScot trades every wellness-category cliché — soft pastels, spa-speak — for a dockside industrial palette and a bespoke display face whose only lowercase-style character is a tiny degree symbol standing in for the letter O. A clean lesson in building a whole concept, hot-to-cold contrast, out of one typographic quirk instead of a moodboard of ice cubes.

No. 06Art Direction

John Patrikas builds pop’s most ambiguous album art

John Patrikas for Addison Rae, Tyla & Steve Lacy

Creative director John Patrikas builds the visual worlds behind several of this year’s most transformative pop album eras, packaging ambiguity and friction into kaleidoscopic covers rather than the usual pop-star formula. Useful viewing for anyone briefing a rebrand around reinvention — his process starts from what an artist feels but can’t yet articulate, not from a reference deck.

No. 07Art Direction

Robert Beatty paints album covers like folk art

Robert Beatty

Beatty’s primeval, retro-Photoshopped album covers favour creative friction over polish, built from layered scans and deliberately degraded textures rather than clean vector work. A reminder that “handmade” doesn’t have to mean film grain — sometimes it means embracing the glitches a tool wants to make.

No. 08Typography

Supersystème treats every letterform as an image

Supersystème

The Montreal studio’s identities, posters and publications treat typography as image first and language second, letting the scale, direction and juxtaposition of letterforms carry as much meaning as the words themselves. Essential viewing for any brief that treats type as a delivery mechanism for copy rather than a subject in its own right.

No. 09Art Direction

Parallel Press rescues Black astronauts from the archive

Parallel Press, ‘Astro Black’

Parallel Press’ Astro Black catalogues the overlooked visual history of Black astronauts through independent book design and archival graphic work, treating preservation itself as a design discipline. A good prompt for thinking about identity design as an act of protecting a story, not just packaging one.

Four things to chew on this week
Jackie Jantos (Hinge) It’s Nice That · In House column · ~12 min read

Hinge’s CEO on inclusive design and the path from creative to CEO

In the second instalment of It’s Nice That’s In House column, Hinge’s CEO explains why she kept ‘Designed to be deleted’ on arrival instead of redoing it, and how she builds product research around the specific communities Hinge serves. A candid look at what it takes for a creative leader to become a category’s default without losing the point of view that got her there.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Dr. Joy Buolamwini Design Matters · Podcast · ~50 min listen

Dr. Joy Buolamwini on unmasking AI and the poetry of code

Debbie Millman talks with the computer scientist and self-described “poet of code” about founding the Algorithmic Justice League and what it means to use art and research to make algorithmic bias visible. Essential listening for any designer whose work increasingly touches AI-generated assets or automated decision systems, whether they’ve clocked it yet or not.

Listen on Design Matters →
Kelli Anderson It’s Nice That · Nicer Tuesdays talk · ~6 min read

Kelli Anderson approaches graphic design as a fine art, one paper gadget at a time

Onstage with Elizabeth Goodspeed, the paper engineer talks through Alphabet in Motion, her new pop-up book of tactile typography, and hints at an upcoming paper record player. A great nudge for any designer whose “craft” has quietly gone entirely screen-based to go build something with their hands again.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Chris Trotman & Anna Burles Creative Boom · Insight · ~7 min read

Inside Wolves of Tokyo: the design lessons behind a Japan-noir restaurant

Run for the Hills develops branding and interiors under one roof rather than handing a finished identity to an interiors team, letting a restaurant’s colour palette come from the room itself before it’s pulled back into the logo. A worthwhile read for anyone whose branding work stops at a guidelines PDF instead of following through into the physical space a client actually occupies.
