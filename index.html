No. 01Brand Identity

Hiut Denim speaks in its makers’ handwriting

Pentagram (Hugh Miller) for Hiut Denim

Pentagram’s Hugh Miller rebrands the Welsh denim label around a wordmark redrawn from the factory floor itself, with a typeface pulled directly from the makers’ own signatures and a rounder, friendlier owl mascot. The case study for any heritage brand wondering how to make “craft” feel less like nostalgia and more like a place a real person works.

No. 02Brand Identity

Adobe’s logotype gets a quiet refresh

Mother Design for Adobe

Mother Design’s new identity system for Adobe pays direct homage to Marva Warnock’s 1982 logotype, refining the letterforms rather than redrawing them and building a wider visual system around the work that already exists. A textbook lesson in how to update an icon without spending the equity it took 40 years to build.

No. 03Brand Identity

TwelveLabs gets a grown-up identity for AI video

Pentagram for TwelveLabs

Pentagram crafts a quietly confident system for AI video startup TwelveLabs that resists the genre’s usual sci-fi gradients in favor of restrained typography and a precise color palette. Useful reference for any AI client whose product is serious enough to deserve a logo that isn’t a sparkle.

No. 04Brand Identity

St Paul’s Cathedral gets its first proper wordmark

Pentagram (Domenic Lippa) for St Paul’s Cathedral

Pentagram’s Domenic Lippa centers the new identity for one of London’s most photographed buildings on a single bespoke wordmark, drawn in a serif that quietly nods to the cathedral’s 300-year history without re-doing it. A reminder that institutional clients usually need less new design than they think, applied with more discipline than they expect.

No. 05Typography

Glacier carves a display face out of Iceland

Omar Careaga (released via Creative Boom roundup)

Glacier is a sharp-shouldered display face inspired by Icelandic terrain — angular where it could be ornamental, with flowing transitions where most display faces would go static. A useful addition to any toolkit that still over-relies on Migra or Reckless for “cold-but-elegant” headlines.

No. 06Typography

La Mericana wakes a 1965 wedge serif up

Released via Creative Boom typography roundup

A vintage-meets-modern family of wedge serifs built on Richard Isbell’s 1965 Americana, with the warmth and generous x-height of the original but cleaned up for screens. A near-perfect substitute the next time a client asks for “something nostalgic but not Cooper Black again.”

No. 07Typography

Reel asks whether a headline has to shout

Jamie Clarke Type

A super-condensed headline face that sets confidently at large sizes without resorting to extra weight or extreme contrast — Clarke designed it for moments when the layout, not the type, should be doing the work. A better default than yet another anonymized grotesque when the brief calls for “editorial but loud.”

Envato adds a voice to its visual mark

Envato in-house team

The creative-asset marketplace launches a new logo paired with a custom sonic identity that’s meant to live across in-product sounds, brand films, and event opens. Worth studying as one of the cleaner recent examples of how a primarily visual brand should commission and deploy its first sound system.

No. 09Brand Identity

Craft rebrands around “cultivation” instead of recruitment

Craft (in-house, via Creative Boom)

Creative-talent agency Craft repositions itself around the language of growing talent rather than placing it, with a visual system built from plant and seed forms instead of the usual roster-of-faces look. A useful tonal shift to study for any service-business client whose category has been visually stuck for a decade.

Four things to chew on this week
Katharina Sussek & Jens Müller It’s Nice That · Book excerpt · ~10 min

From Olivetti to Instagram: a short history of modern brand design

An excerpt from Taschen’s new The Elements of Brand Design that walks through how brand identity actually got to where it is — Persil’s deliberately green packaging in 1907, Olivetti’s mid-century coherence, the algorithmic feed of 2026. A useful corrective whenever a deck pretends a trend started this quarter.

Motto (interview) The Brand Identity · Interview · ~15 min

Inside the Future of Brand 2026 report with Motto’s co-founders

Sunny Bonnell and Ashleigh Hansberger walk through Motto’s new annual report on where brand-building is going next — the shift toward fewer, sharper bets and away from omnichannel sprawl. Worth a careful read before any 2027 planning conversation that’s about to invent a five-pillar framework.

Creative Boom Editorial · Essay · ~6 min

How being weird can save branding in 2026

An argument that the current sea of optimized, AI-flattened identities has created a real commercial opening for brands willing to look strange on purpose — and a defense of the kinds of decisions that don’t survive a stakeholder grid. The pitch-deck-ready case for any client who keeps asking you to “make it more like everyone else.”
