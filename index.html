No. 01Brand Identity

4Creative’s new logo is written by its whole team

In-house — 4Creative (UK), May 2026

Channel 4’s creative arm built its new identity around a single rule: each C is drawn by a different team member, generating 130+ unique marks that rotate through the system. The identity is literally a record of who made it. Keep this one for any brand that claims “people are our product” but has yet to let those people into the mark itself.

Uncommon Creative Studio for OFFF Barcelona 2026

No. 03Brand Identity

Huckberry evolves after seven years of holding still

The Brand Identity — Huckberry in-house, May 2026

After nearly a decade with the same system, Huckberry asked not “is this current?” but “will this still feel right in 2030?” The answer was evolution, not revolution: Larken (organic serif), F37 Blanka (Swiss sans), and GT Alpina Typewriter (editorial texture) form a three-typeface hierarchy. The Huckberry tree — on every package they ship — was treated as untouchable. A useful discipline for any multi-sub-brand client wary of wholesale change.

No. 04Art Direction

The Met Gala turns the red carpet into a live gallery

The Metropolitan Museum of Art — Costume Institute 2026

The “Fashion Is Art” / “Costume Art” dress code produced the most rigorously argued Met Gala in years: garments directly referencing Warhol, Klimt, ancient Greek sculpture, and Surrealism, treated less as looks than as curatorial statements. Relevant beyond fashion — a live example of how a tight exhibition concept can function as a global visual brief when the framing is precise enough.

No. 05Brand Identity

Praktika builds its grid from the floor up

Praktika for NAI — architecture identity, Lithuania, Apr 28

The grid governing Praktika’s identity is lifted directly from traditional Lithuanian floor tiles — the repeating unit carrying both spatial logic (architecture) and cultural memory (the tile’s pre-modern vernacular). The system scales from business card to building signage without modification. Useful precedent for any heritage or place-rooted client asking how to make “local” feel structural rather than decorative.

No. 07Campaign

Ayo Edebiri is Loewe’s last image under Anderson

Loewe — Spring/Summer 2026 campaign

Edebiri as “the ultimate Loewe girl” — soft-spoken confidence, cerebral, slightly anti-glossy — is the purest distillation of Jonathan Anderson’s decade-long argument that luxury should feel inhabited, not aspirational. No. 09Campaign

Chanel casts five faces and lets none dominate

Chanel — Eyewear SS2026, creative dir. Matthieu Blazy

Nicole Kidman, G-Dragon, Lily-Rose Depp, Pedro Pascal, and Ayo Edebiri — five radically different energy signatures, equal billing. Blazy’s casting philosophy made visible: no single ambassador who “is” the brand, but an ensemble that argues plurality as the luxury position. Four things to chew on this week
Elizabeth Goodspeed It’s Nice That · Column · ~8 min read

Why design writing needs designers writing

Goodspeed’s argument: when designers write, their essays and their portfolios are circling the same question from two directions — that’s a feature, not a conflict. She traces how the best design criticism has always come from practitioners and asks why the discipline has outsourced its own explanation to people who don’t draw. A useful provocation if you’ve been meaning to write about your work but aren’t sure it “counts.”

The Conversation Academic op-ed · Essay · ~5 min read

The 2026 Met Gala dress code is ‘Fashion Is Art.’ But is it?

The best post-Gala essay you’ll find: a dress historian unpacks exactly when fashion stopped being a craft category and started being claimed as fine art, and whether the Met Gala’s version of that argument is honest or simply aspirational branding for the Costume Institute. Pair with item 04 for a complete read on what it means for a cultural institution to use its own programming as image strategy.

Debbie Millman Design Matters · Podcast · ~65 min

Design Matters: 20th Anniversary — The Most Memorable Interviews

Millman assembles the conversations that changed how she thinks — Rick Rubin on making without audience, Marina Abramovic on duration, Roxane Gay on voice. Worth listening to not as a greatest-hits clip reel but as a document of taste: what a designer chose to preserve tells you more about her values than any portfolio. Lifestyle.INQ / Inquirer Essay · Industry analysis · ~6 min read

Fashion’s campaign reset: what the Versace three-photographer model tells us about luxury in 2026

An analysis of why Versace split its SS26 campaign across three photographers — Meisel, Lebon, and Tania Franco Klein — rather than the single-vision approach that defined luxury advertising for 40 years. The argument: “a brand” is no longer one aesthetic but a set of tensions deliberately held together. Useful framing for any multi-SKU or multi-audience brief where “consistency” is being used as a cover for creative timidity.
