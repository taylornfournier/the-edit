No. 01Brand Identity

The Click puts the I in 24 disciplines

The Click for Imperial College London

Following Pentagram's 2024 rebrand, The Click built out a modular sub-identity for all 24 faculties — each gets a bespoke "I" letterform whose interior pattern encodes its subject: DNA strands for Life Sciences, molecular chains for Chemistry, gliding geometry for Aeronautics. A masterclass in scalable identity architecture: the system can grow forever, but every mark is still unmistakably Imperial.

No. 02Typography

Hellcare Regular makes illegibility the point

Parker Jones & Rajshree Saraf / Wieden+Kennedy NY

A typeface built from actual doctors' handwriting — each letter engineered to look rushed, chaotic, and unreadable — designed as a critique of the American healthcare system's inaccessibility. The brief: make illegibility feel systematic, not accidental. A rare example of craft in service of argument; download it and keep it for the client pitch where you need to explain why concept has to precede form.

No. 03Brand Identity

Smith & Diction brands the orbital ranch

Smith & Diction for Cowboy Space Corp

A spur that doubles as a sun rising over Earth's horizon, set against a Joshua Tree-inspired palette that refuses all the clichés of "space-y." Built in days for a $275M announcement, which is the real lesson: fast projects reveal what you actually believe, not what you can research into the ground. The full case study on Medium is required reading for anyone juggling speed and strategy.

No. 04Brand Identity

Mother flips Adobe's A from void to solid

Mother Design for Adobe

Four decades of the negative-space "A" — quietly inverted into a filled, confident mark alongside a custom display typeface evolved from the original Warnock family. Mother also introduced "The Lens," a frame device used to contain and transform imagery across the system. A textbook lesson in how to evolve legacy equity rather than abandon it; worth bookmarking every time a client insists "we need to start from scratch."

No. 05Brand Identity

Sprite puts the Lymon back in the dot

forpeople for Sprite / The Coca-Cola Company

A global rollout across 180 markets that retrofits Sprite's Lymon symbol — the lemon-lime hybrid icon dropped years ago — back into the wordmark as the dot on the lowercase i, plus a new vertical can layout and a sonic identity co-created with Mustard. The move to make a heritage symbol load-bearing again (rather than decorative) is the strategic lesson worth carrying into beverage and CPG work.

No. 06Brand Identity

Lacoste doubles down on its Frenchness

Commission Studio for Lacoste

Commission Studio — known for Tiffany, Rimowa, Fenty — restores a heritage serif to Lacoste's wordmark and re-anchors the crocodile in its original proportions, after a survey revealed most consumers didn't know the brand was French. A case study in brief clarity: when a brand discovers a specific gap in perception, the design response should address exactly that gap, and nothing else.

No. 07Campaign

Pentagram covers faces to make women visible

Marina Willer / Pentagram — UNSEEN campaign

A red dot placed over each portrait's face — simultaneously obscuring the subject and making her impossible to ignore — across 68 Manhattan newsstands and Times Square. Marina Willer's campaign for North Korean women's rights is one of the most economical visual arguments I've seen this year: one symbol, one gesture, one idea. Study it for any social-impact brief where the design has to do persuasion at scale, fast.

No. 08Packaging

Made Thought makes CBD glow without the wellness clichés

Made Thought for Juana Skin

A luminescent yellow-green palette and misty gradients that feel simultaneously organic and scientific, designed for a Dubai-based CBD skincare brand that needed to look authoritative in markets where the ingredient is still finding its footing. Made Thought's move — choosing to suggest rather than explain — is the right one whenever a brand has to navigate regulatory ambiguity without looking apologetic.

No. 09Art Direction

Versace hires three photographers and means it

Dario Vitale for Versace SS26 — Meisel / Lebon / Franco Klein

Three radically different lenses — Meisel's overhead group glamour, Lebon's collage-and-chemical-distortion street energy, Klein's saturated cinematic darkness — made to cohere as a single campaign. Dario Vitale's final collection at Versace, and the most interesting art direction argument for multi-photographer campaigns since Juergen Teller started doing the same for Vivienne Westwood.

No. 10Brand Identity

4Creative builds an identity that can never be finished

4Creative — self-initiated rebrand

Channel 4's in-house agency redesigned its own identity around a logo that is structurally incomplete — each new staff member adds their own bespoke 4C icon to the system — while the wider visual vocabulary draws on cutout icons from decades of Channel 4's most beloved shows. A rare example of an agency identity that actually argues something about the agency's culture, not just its portfolio.

Elizabeth Goodspeed It's Nice That · Column · ~8 min read

"What aesthetic is this?" — On the push to categorise visual culture

Goodspeed's latest asks what gets lost when every image becomes searchable by aesthetic category — when work is compressed into labels like "dark academia" or "quiet luxury" before it has a chance to mean something else. The argument: categorisation is a convenience for platforms, not a vocabulary for creators. Read it before your next moodboarding session and you'll pull references differently.
