No. 01Brand Identity

Instagram lets its decade-old wordmark exhale

Christy Silva & Cynthia Pratomo for Instagram

Instagram’s first wordmark change in over a decade trades a thin, over-explained script for a heavier, more confident cursive built to read as a grown brand rather than a new one still justifying itself. A useful study in what a decade of equity actually buys a brand: permission to stop over-communicating and start simply being.

Accept & Proceed for Vans

Accept & Proceed rebuilds Vans’ identity system around the scuffed, hand-taped ephemera of actual skate culture rather than a polished heritage-brand retrospective. A reminder that authenticity in a legacy rebrand often means resisting the urge to clean everything up.

Porto Rocha steps back so the art speaks

Porto Rocha for Almeida & Dale

For a 1998 São Paulo gallery, Porto Rocha builds an identity confident enough to stay quiet, letting a flexible mark and restrained palette frame the art instead of competing with it. Useful proof that in gallery and culture work, the most memorable move is sometimes the most editorial restraint.

Studio Blackburn unfolds Brompton’s 50-year story

Studio Blackburn for Brompton

Under the platform ‘Life Unfolded,’ Studio Blackburn builds a global system flexible enough to move between engineering heritage, retail and advertising without diluting Brompton’s Britishness into cliché. Samar Maakaroun designs a family, not one brand

Samar Maakaroun for Sada Mada

Pentagram partner Samar Maakaroun builds Sada Mada as an umbrella system flexible enough to hold a growing family of creative ventures rather than one fixed identity. No. 06Art Direction

Marina Willer scores an identity from sound

Marina Willer for Muziekcentrum De Bijloke

Ahead of a new concert season, Pentagram’s Marina Willer builds Ghent’s De Bijloke a ‘living identity’ drawn from oscilloscope waveforms and John Cage’s notation systems, so the graphic language shifts with whatever is playing that night. A striking example of translating an intangible experience, live sound, into a flexible system instead of a static logo.

No. 07Art Direction

Sam Wenc paints record covers entirely by hand

Sam Wenc, Lobby Art Editions

Wenc’s hand-painted album covers for independent musicians skip digital tools almost entirely, treating each release as a one-off object rather than a repeatable template. A good nudge for any designer whose “craft” has quietly gone entirely screen-based to remember what a client notices when something is visibly made by hand.

No. 08Campaign

Steven Meisel frames Dolce & Gabbana’s oldest codes

Steven Meisel for Dolce & Gabbana

For its FW26 campaign, Dolce & Gabbana hands its most foundational codes — Mediterranean lace, sharp tailoring, theatrical masculinity — to Steven Meisel, who reframes decades of house signatures as a live dialogue between two collections rather than a nostalgia trip. Worth studying for how a heritage brand keeps its codes legible without simply repeating them season after season.

Five things to chew on this week
Elizabeth Goodspeed It’s Nice That · Column · ~9 min read

How a municipal grocery store became the design world’s hottest RFP

Goodspeed digs into Mayor Zohran Mamdani’s city-run grocery pilot to ask what happens when civic branding has to signal institutional trust and populist warmth at once, a tension most city-hall identity work never has to hold. A sharp companion for anyone doing civic or public-sector work who’s been asked to make something feel “approachable” and “credible” in the same breath.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Creative Boom Editorial Creative Boom · Insight · ~6 min read

Creatives are deeply divided over the new Instagram logo, and I think that points to a broader issue

Published the same week It’s Nice That ran its inside look at the redesign (above), this piece argues the split reaction says less about the wordmark than about how differently designers and the public read “finished” work. Read alongside the feature, it’s a reminder that shipping a rebrand is never the end of the story — the discourse is part of the brand now too.

Read on Creative Boom →
Victoria Montgomery & Tamira Hamden Creative Boom · Insight · ~8 min read

‘We’re not here to beg for a seat’: how Victoria Montgomery & Tamira Hamden built Women in Brand

The founders talk through building a community specifically for women working in brand strategy and identity design, a corner of the industry that’s historically under-represented at the senior level even as the work itself skews heavily female. Worth reading for the practical model as much as the mission: how a niche professional community builds real influence without waiting for an invitation from the top.

Read on Creative Boom →
Nosheen Ahmed Creative Boom · Insight · ~7 min read

How Nosheen Ahmed lost her side business to AI, and bet her whole career on the ‘human hand’

Ahmed recounts watching an AI-generated knockoff undercut her illustration side-hustle overnight, and the deliberate pivot that followed toward work that’s explicitly, visibly hand-made and priced accordingly. A grounding read for any designer quietly wondering which parts of their own practice are actually AI-resistant, and which just haven’t been targeted yet.

Read on Creative Boom →
Kimberly Parry The Futur · Podcast · ~55 min listen

Kimberly Parry on clothing, confidence and the architecture of personal identity

Stylist and “archetypal coach” Kimberly Parry walks Chris Do through her Succeed with Style framework, unpacking why people sabotage a new look the moment they try it on and how her Bro Pro, Faux Pro and GOAT archetypes work as a personal-branding exercise before anyone touches a closet. A worthwhile listen for brand strategists: it’s the same identity-first thinking client work runs on, just applied to one person instead of a company.
