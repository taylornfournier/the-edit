No. 01Brand Identity

Tinder trades lowercase intimacy for a serif voice

Porto Rocha for Tinder

For its first major identity change in a decade, Porto Rocha drops Tinder’s soft lowercase wordmark for a confident all-caps serif and widens the palette from a murky red-orange-pink gradient into a clearer spread of red, blue and green. A useful case study in how much editorial personality a product-first, interface-heavy brand can gain just by giving its type a point of view.

No. 02Brand Identity

Hovis and Kingsmill merge without a wheat sheaf in sight

BrandOpus for Hovis Bakeries

Rather than lean on bread’s usual visual shorthand of wheat, sunrises and gingham, BrandOpus builds the newly merged Hovis, Kingsmill, Allinson’s and Sunblest around the four elements coming together to make bread, giving four heritage brands one shared idea instead of a flattened logo mash-up. A sharp reference for any merger brief tempted to reach for the obvious category cliché first.

No. 03Brand Identity

Wottz hides a lightning bolt in its own crossbar

Lark Design Studio for Wottz

Lark swaps the EV charging brand’s looping green cable-shapes and generic blue wordmark for a sharpened logotype that tucks a tiny lightning strike into the crossbar of its two Ts, letting one confident letterform do the work six decorative elements used to. Proof that a single, precise idea can read as more premium than a whole moodboard of borrowed cues.

No. 04Brand Identity

Stack Overflow rebuilds itself around human proof

Koto for Stack Overflow

As AI chatbots increasingly answer the questions Stack Overflow’s forums used to, Koto repositions the platform around what those bots can’t fake: verified, human, community-tested knowledge, expressed through offset “stack” building blocks and a generative identity system built with Claude. An early, well-argued answer to the question every knowledge brand is quietly sitting with right now.

No. 05Art Direction

Charli xcx poses with her own trinity of icons

Aidan Zamiri for Charli xcx, ‘Music, Fashion, Film’

For the alternate cover of her seventh album, Charli steps into the black-and-white frame alongside John Cale, Marc Jacobs and Martin Scorsese, the three “legends” the original cover paid tribute to without including her. No. 06Campaign

Chanel hands its sixty-year-old Flora to Gracie Abrams

Chanel, starring Gracie Abrams

To launch Coco Mademoiselle Crush Absolu, Chanel casts the singer as the new face of a fragrance built on its Coco Crush jewelry line, continuing the house’s pattern of pairing heritage assets with a single, culturally fluent young ambassador rather than a full ensemble cast. A clean reminder that legacy motifs often need recasting more than reinventing.

No. 07Typography

Oratorio revives a 1970s type showman’s flair

Alex Chavot for Apex Type Foundry

Chavot’s new single-weight display serif takes 1910’s Halbfette Cantate as its skeleton and runs it through the theatrical swashes and high contrast of Ed Benguiat’s flamboyant 1970s work, arriving just as most foundries chase flexible variable families instead. A good argument for why one fully-committed weight can still say more than an entire adjustable system.

No. 08Art Direction

A scanner becomes Armand Croisonnier’s memory machine

Armand Croisonnier

The Paris-based artist feeds friends, objects and himself through a flatbed scanner, letting its cold glass balloon, flatten and slice his subjects into a squishy, distorted archive built from grief and the urge to preserve what’s already disappearing. A technique worth stealing for any brief asking for “handmade” texture without reaching for the usual film-grain shortcut.

No. 09Campaign

A designer turns the maths of motherhood into posters

Rachel Many, ‘The Mother Load’

The Los Angeles designer pairs a series of sharp, funny, angry posters with a data-backed essay on what having children costs creative women specifically — working mothers earn 74.3 cents to every dollar fathers make, and childless women get twice the callbacks mothers with identical résumés do. A rare self-initiated project doing real advocacy through pure graphic clarity instead of illustration or sentiment.

Four things to chew on this week
Loredana Crisan (Figma) It’s Nice That · In House column · ~12 min read

Figma’s Loredana Crisan talks taste and last-minute decisions

In the debut of It’s Nice That’s new In House column, Figma’s chief design officer defines taste as care — for the person on the other side of the screen, for the details almost no one sees — and admits Config’s line-up isn’t locked until the weekend before it opens. A candid look at how a design leader actually prioritizes under pressure, rather than the tidy version that makes it into keynote slides.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Matter Of It’s Nice That · Publication spotlight · ~10 min read

Are we stuck in 20th-century typography?

German studio Matter Of’s book ‘New New Typography’ deliberately refuses to be an encyclopedia, manual or history, instead pulling together nearly 100 collaborators and 243 figures — from 1602 illustrated letterforms to type generated by scanning the human body — into a messy, unstable argument against tidy typographic rules. A genuinely useful jolt for anyone whose type choices have quietly calcified into habit.

Read on It’s Nice That →
Laverne Cox Design Matters · Podcast · ~50 min listen

Laverne Cox on survival, healing and becoming yourself

Recorded live at New York’s Pride Book Fair, Debbie Millman talks with the Emmy-winning actor and advocate about her new memoir Transcendent, and what it takes to define yourself in a world determined to do it for you. Less a design conversation than a masterclass in the storytelling and self-presentation that sits underneath every strong brand narrative.

Listen on Design Matters →
Jarrett Fuller Scratching the Surface · Newsletter · ~8 min read

The power of print refuses to go away

In this month’s dispatch, Fuller argues that printed, physical objects still carry a unique power to shape narratives, critique and brand in a way screens can’t fully replace, and predicts more experimentation with print over the next year, not less. A grounding read for anyone whose portfolio has drifted entirely into pixels.
